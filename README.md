# LandRecord

**LandRecord** is an independent Indian land-record and land-services information platform.

It helps users understand land records, maps, documents, procedures, calculators, revenue services and related information in simple Hindi + English, while directing users to appropriate official sources.

> **Important:** LandRecord is not a government website and must never imply government affiliation or endorsement.

## 🧭 Master Product Roadmap

The goal is to build LandRecord as a modern India-wide land-information and utility platform that is more useful and easier to navigate than fragmented government portals and thin private SEO sites.

### Product position
LandRecord is:
- an independent land-information discovery platform
- a state-aware service navigator
- a land-record knowledge base
- a map and location discovery layer
- a calculator/tool platform
- a Smart Land Assistant / Rule Engine
- a future foundation for legitimate assisted services

LandRecord is not:
- a government portal
- a property broker
- a law firm
- a replacement for official records
- a reason to publish millions of thin URLs

### Competitive product strategy
Competitor research shows three major patterns:
1. Government portals have authoritative services and records but can be fragmented and difficult to navigate.
2. Private SEO sites often answer individual searches but are fragmented and may provide limited utility.
3. Property-verification/assistance businesses demonstrate future commercial demand for legitimate land-document assistance.

LandRecord should combine the useful parts without copying branding:
- official-source discovery
- state-specific service knowledge
- modern UX
- natural-language search
- Rule Engine
- maps
- calculators
- document checklists
- related-service navigation
- source/review transparency

Paid services are intentionally deferred until the free platform has meaningful traffic, trust, validated workflows and appropriate authorization.

## 🎨 Master Design System Direction

The design target is a premium, modern land-tech / information utility, not a government-portal clone and not a generic SEO blog.

### Visual principles
- Clean, trustworthy and calm.
- Strong information hierarchy.
- Mobile-first.
- Spacious but not wasteful.
- Limited card-wall usage.
- Clear primary actions.
- Large tap targets.
- Subtle borders/elevation rather than excessive decoration.
- Minimal animation.
- Fast rendering.
- Full light/dark theme support.
- Hindi and English typography must remain readable.
- Every public page must feel like the same product.

### Global navigation
Desktop:
- Brand/logo.
- Primary destinations: Explore, Services, Maps, Tools, Guides.
- Prominent search.
- Language control.
- Theme control.

Mobile:
- Brand.
- Search always accessible.
- Hindi/English control visible.
- Theme control visible.
- Primary destinations such as Records, Maps, Mutation and Tools remain visible in a compact horizontal or action navigation.
- Secondary navigation can expand.
- Never rely on a hamburger as the only way to discover core services.

### Homepage design structure
1. Hero — clear value proposition, natural-language search and example queries.
2. Quick Actions — Land Records, Maps, Mutation, Property Records, Measurement, Registration, Land Guidance.
3. Smart Land Assistant — user describes the goal in ordinary language; Rule Engine provides a state-aware route.
4. Explore by State — searchable/scrollable state discovery with service availability.
5. Map Explorer — India → State → District → Tehsil/Taluka with official map discovery.
6. Popular Records & Services — genuinely useful high-intent destinations without an uncontrolled card wall.
7. Tools — area/unit conversion, measurement utilities, document checklists and reliable calculators.
8. Guides / Problem Solver — user-problem-first content and state-specific instructions.
9. Recently Reviewed — source/review freshness for trust.
10. Trust — independent-platform disclosure, official-source links, source/review metadata and limitations.

### Service/record page pattern
1. Breadcrumb
2. H1 + concise Hindi/English explanation
3. Jurisdiction/state scope
4. What this record/service means
5. Who needs it / common use cases
6. How to access or use the official service
7. Required information/documents
8. Official portal/source
9. Rule Engine / next-step CTA where relevant
10. Map/location context
11. Useful tools
12. Related services
13. FAQ where genuinely useful
14. Source + last reviewed + limitations

### Search experience
Search must support both exact terms such as 7/12, Jamabandi and Property Card and natural-language intent such as “Maharashtra mein zameen ka record dekhna hai”.

Results should be grouped by:

### Map UX

## FINAL COMPETITOR & FEATURE-GAP RESEARCH — 03 OCT 2026

This research is a product-planning benchmark, not a claim that LandRecord should copy any competitor.

### 1. Government ecosystem benchmark

DILRMP's current State/UT RoR directory shows that land-record systems are fragmented by jurisdiction. Examples include Maharashtra Mahabhumi, Karnataka Bhoomi, Uttar Pradesh Bhulekh, Bihar Bhoomi, Rajasthan Apna Khata, Telangana Bhu Bharati, Odisha Bhulekh and other state systems. The directory also distinguishes RoR, BhuNaksha, land-record and property-registration systems.

Observed common capabilities:
- State/district/tehsil/village hierarchy
- Record-of-Rights search
- owner/survey/plot/khata based search depending on state
- cadastral/map access in some states
- mutation/namantaran workflows
- application/status tracking in some states
- registration/document workflows in some integrated systems
- grievance/correction workflows
- digitally signed records in supported jurisdictions
- notices, court/revenue information and other state-specific services

Examples verified during research:
- Maharashtra Mahabhulekh exposes 7/12, 8A, Property Card and K-Prat search, with district/taluka/village and survey/name inputs.
- Uttar Pradesh Bhulekh exposes Khatauni, certified-copy payment, sale status, litigation status and plot/gata-related records.
- Bihar Bhoomi exposes digital signed records, online mutation, land-revenue payment, revenue courts, e-Mapi, land-use conversion, map delivery, SMS alerts, correction and mortgage/record information.
- Rajasthan Apna Khata exposes Jamabandi, mutation applications/status, Seemagyan, maps and related agricultural land-record workflows.
- Telangana Bhu Bharati exposes land-detail search, registered-document lookup, GIS parcel information and grievance workflows.

### 2. Private/product benchmark

Two product directions are especially relevant.

Land intelligence / verification:
Current Maharashtra-focused products such as BhuMe and LandSahi combine official-record discovery with map views, record aggregation, mutation/deed analysis, risk checks or monitoring. Their positioning demonstrates that users may value a workflow that connects several official records rather than reading one record in isolation.

SEO information sites:
Independent guides typically answer one narrow question at a time: what a record means, how to find it, which portal to use, what fields mean and what to do next. This is useful for search acquisition but often leaves the user to connect multiple pages and portals themselves.

### 3. Product gap identified

The strongest gap is not another record-viewing portal. Official systems already perform record retrieval.

LandRecord should focus on the layer between:
User goal → terminology → correct jurisdiction → correct service → official source → explanation → next action

Core differentiators to build:
1. Natural-language land search
2. State-aware terminology mapping
3. Explainable Rule Engine
4. Problem Solver journeys
5. Official-source discovery with source/review metadata
6. Cross-linking between records, maps, mutation, registration and tools
7. Document/readiness checklists
8. Map-to-service discovery
9. Land-record terminology assistant
10. State-specific process navigation
11. Related-service engine
12. Future saved checklists/monitoring only after reliable data architecture exists
