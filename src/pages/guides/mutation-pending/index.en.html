Recording a new name in the revenue record after a sale or inheritance (dakhil-kharij, namantaran, intkal, ferfar) sometimes gets stuck. Below are general steps. Time limits, fees and appeals depend on state law, which we have not verified here.

1. First confirm the application was registered

  • Keep the acknowledgement/receipt or online application number.
  • Check the status on the state portal or at the tehsil. If no number exists, the application may never have been registered.

2. Look for common reasons

  • Khasra, khata, area or name in the deed/succession papers not matching the record.
  • An objection or dispute has been filed.
  • Not all heirs are named in an inheritance case.
  • A required report or inquiry is incomplete.

3. Fix the gaps

If the office points out a missing document or correction, ask for it in writing and get a receipt. For name spelling differences see the correction guide.

4. Go to a higher revenue officer

If the application is pending without reason, you can write to a higher officer in the same department; which officer sits at which level varies by state. Attach the application copy and acknowledgement. If there is an objection or dispute, consult a lawyer or the revenue court process.

5. RTI and grievance portals

Right to Information or state grievance portals may be available to learn an application's status and the reason for delay. Check rules and fees on the relevant government site.

Meanwhile

  • Keep proof of possession and cultivation (receipts, photos).
  • It is safer to postpone big transactions until mutation is complete.

An example

Three months after registration the name was still not in the khatauni. The acknowledgement and the portal status showed "objection pending", meaning someone had objected. The right step then is to ask for the reason in writing and prepare a reply, not just to keep visiting the office.
