Under the central government's AgriStack initiative, every state is building a Farmer Registry. Each farmer gets an Aadhaar-based Farmer ID and the land in their name is linked from the state land record. Land links only when your name is recorded correctly in the land record, so checking the record is the first step.

Key points from official material

  • States build it: registries are compiled by states on common standards; the Centre keeps a copy.
  • Farmer ID vs Farm ID: Farmer ID identifies the person; Farm ID identifies the farmer's land parcel within a survey/sub-survey number.
  • How land is linked: the farmer searches one plot, picks their name from the list of owners, it is checked against the record of rights (RoR), and they give consent by e-sign. All of a farmer's land in a village appears as one "bucket". Where the record is already Aadhaar-seeded, land can show up automatically after eKYC.
  • Name matching: per the FAQ, both the farmer's and father's names are matched; large differences send the claim for an official's approval.
  • Use: per AgriStack, the link is for non-legal uses such as schemes, advisory and planning, not for deciding ownership.

Check your land record before registering

Most problems are in the land record, not the registry. Before registering:

  1. Open your khatauni/jamabandi/7-12 on your state's portal.
  2. Check that your name and father's name are written as in Aadhaar. Even spelling differences can block the match.
  3. If inheritance was never recorded after a parent's or grandparent's death, the land is still in their name: see the inheritance guide.
  4. If mutation of purchased land is pending, finish it first: mutation stuck.
  5. For wrong names or areas, follow the correction process.

Where registration happens

Each state has its own Farmer Registry site, usually on an agristack.gov.in sub-domain (for example Bihar's bhfr.agristack.gov.in and Gujarat's gjfr.agristack.gov.in). Many states also help through village camps, CSCs or agriculture/revenue staff. Ask the agriculture department, patwari/lekhpal or CSC which route is open in your state, and always use a .gov.in link.

Avoid scams

  • Don't give OTPs, Aadhaar or bank details to unknown people "making Farmer IDs".
  • Look-alike private apps are not government apps; take links from the state's official site.
  • A Farmer ID does not make land yours or anyone else's; title documents stay the same.

Relation to Bhu-Aadhaar (ULPIN)

Bhu-Aadhaar is a national ID for a land parcel generated from map coordinates, while Farm ID identifies a farmer's land within the registry. They serve different purposes; see what is Bhu-Aadhaar.

Example

A farmer's father has died but the khatauni still shows the father's name. Searching the land in the registry, the farmer's name is not among the owners, so the claim fails. The right step is to get the inheritance recorded at the tehsil first, then link the land again once the new record appears.
