An Encumbrance Certificate (EC) shows which registered transactions took place on a piece of land or property during a chosen period, such as sales, mortgages, gifts or leases. It is one of the papers most often asked for when buying land or taking a bank loan against it.

What an EC shows

  • Every document registered on the property in the chosen period: date, document number, type, parties and sometimes value.
  • If nothing registered is found, a "Nil" EC is issued.

What an EC does not show

This is the most important part, because people treat a Nil EC as complete safety:

  • Unregistered deals: verbal deals, unregistered agreements or possession letters don't appear.
  • Revenue record: whose name is in the khatauni/jamabandi and whether mutation was done is not in the EC. Check the state land record separately.
  • Court cases and stays: a pending civil or revenue case or a stay order may not appear.
  • Possession: see for yourself who occupies the land.

Where and how to get an EC

  1. Find which sub-registrar office covers the property.
  2. Check your state's registration department portal; many states offer EC search/applications online. According to PIB, over 92% of sub-registrar offices were computerised by 2023, so recent records are often online.
  3. Enter property details (village, survey/khasra number, area or an earlier document number) and the period, and pay the fee.
  4. If older years are not digitised, a manual search at the sub-registrar office may be needed.

When reading the EC

  • Match each entry with the seller's chain of documents: after each sale, is the next seller the same person?
  • If a mortgage appears, check that its release/discharge is also registered.
  • Survey/khasra numbers and area should match the deed.

Example

A plot's EC came back "Nil", but in the village it turned out the seller had earlier taken an advance from someone else on an unregistered agreement. The EC did not show it. That is why the khatauni, possession on the ground and asking neighbours matter alongside the EC: checks before buying.
