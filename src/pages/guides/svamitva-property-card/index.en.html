Village houses and the inhabited (abadi) land around them often had no written record of rights. Under the central SVAMITVA scheme, village abadi areas are surveyed by drone and a property card is made for each house/plot. In Uttar Pradesh this rural residential record is called Gharauni; other states may use other names.

Key points from official material

  • Launch: 24 April 2020 (National Panchayati Raj Day), by the Ministry of Panchayati Raj.
  • How: drone surveys and CORS (Continuously Operating Reference Stations) produce high-accuracy maps of abadi areas. Survey of India does the drone survey and maps; states do ground truthing and prepare and distribute the cards.
  • Aim: give owners of village residential land a record of rights, reduce boundary disputes and help raise bank credit on the papers.
  • Progress (to 19 March 2026, PIB): drone survey done in 3.29 lakh of 3.44 lakh targeted villages; 3.10 crore property cards prepared for 1.87 lakh villages and 2.65 crore distributed. Uttar Pradesh: 1.15 crore cards prepared and 1.01 crore distributed (to 11 March 2026).
  • Women's co-ownership: per PIB, some states such as Madhya Pradesh, Haryana, Karnataka, Mizoram, Chhattisgarh, J&K, Puducherry and Daman & Diu/Dadra & Nagar Haveli provide for women's co-ownership on the cards.

How the process usually runs

  1. An announcement in the village and lime (chuna) marking of house boundaries.
  2. Drone survey and map preparation.
  3. Ground verification: revenue/panchayat staff record each property's owner and boundary.
  4. Publication of the list/map and objections; disputes settled through the state's process.
  5. Property cards prepared and distributed; many states also give a digital copy.

Stage names and timing can differ by state. Uttar Pradesh shows details on the state SVAMITVA portal (svamitva.up.gov.in) dashboard.

What to watch during the survey

  • Be present when the team comes and point out your boundary; if a neighbour disputes it, get it recorded in writing then.
  • Have your name spelt as in Aadhaar and other papers.
  • In a joint-family house, settle within the family whose names go on the card.
  • Check the published list; file a written objection before the objection period ends.

Difference from farmland

The property card covers the village's inhabited land. The farm record stays in the khatauni/jamabandi/7-12 as before: how to read a khatauni. In cities, the "property card" of states like Maharashtra is a separate system: CTS property card.

Example

A family's courtyard showed up in the neighbour's share in the survey. As soon as the list was published they gave a written objection to the panchayat and lekhpal with old photos and witnesses, and kept the receipt. After verification the boundary was corrected. Had they waited until cards were distributed, correction would have been harder.
