Bhu-Aadhaar, officially the ULPIN (Unique Land Parcel Identification Number), is a separate identity number for every land parcel. According to the central Department of Land Resources it is a 14-character alphanumeric number generated from the latitude-longitude of the parcel's corners. The government explains it this way: what Aadhaar is to a person, ULPIN is to land.

What the government has said

  • Programme: launched under the Digital India Land Records Modernisation Programme (DILRMP).
  • How it is generated: from the coordinates of each corner (vertex) of the parcel on the state's surveyed, geo-referenced maps. It rests on the land's real location, not on village or district codes.
  • Standards: follows the international ECCMA and OGC standards so all states can adopt one method.
  • What it links: per the official description, ownership details, size and coordinates of the plot.
  • Stated benefits: help in confirming ownership, identifying government land, sharing records across departments and banks, fewer boundary disputes, and disaster planning.

Where it is in use

PIB's December 2023 review said 29 States/UTs had adopted it by then: Andhra Pradesh, Jharkhand, Goa, Bihar, Odisha, Sikkim, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Haryana, Tripura, Chhattisgarh, Jammu & Kashmir, Assam, Madhya Pradesh, Nagaland, Mizoram, Tamil Nadu, Punjab, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu, Himachal Pradesh, West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Kerala, Ladakh, Chandigarh, Karnataka and Delhi. Pilots had been done in Puducherry, Andaman & Nicobar, Manipur and Telangana; work was under way in Arunachal Pradesh and yet to start in Meghalaya. A ministry document from October 2024 also says 29 States/UTs.

Note: "adopted by the state" does not mean every plot in every village has a number. Where the village map is not yet geo-referenced, the number comes later. Check your state portal or tehsil for the current position.

Where to find your Bhu-Aadhaar

We could not confirm a single national government site for ULPIN lookup. Land is a state subject, so the number sits in the state land record. The usual route:

  1. Open your state's official portal (look for gov.in or nic.in in the address bar).
  2. Choose the khatauni/jamabandi/7-12/RoR option, then district, tehsil and village.
  3. Search by khasra/survey number, khata number or name. If you don't know the number, see the guide to finding your khasra number.
  4. Look for a field such as "ULPIN", "Bhu-Aadhaar", "Unique ID" or "Parcel ID" on the record.
  5. If it isn't there, ask the patwari/lekhpal or tehsil whether ULPIN has been generated for your village.

What the portal shows is usually for viewing only. For a certified copy see online vs certified copy.

Avoid scams

  • Bhu-Aadhaar is not a separate "card" that a private site has to make for you. We found no government provision for a citizen application or fee.
  • Don't give your Aadhaar number, OTP or bank details to unknown people or sites in the name of "linking Bhu-Aadhaar".
  • Having a ULPIN does not by itself prove ownership. Title is judged from the sale deed, mutation and revenue record.

An example

Someone hears that "land will get an Aadhaar too", and a website offers to make a "Bhu-Aadhaar card" for ₹500. The right step: open your record on the state's official portal first. If a ULPIN is shown, that is your number and nobody needs to be paid for it. If not, ask the tehsil how far the village map digitisation has progressed.
