Registering the sale deed is the key step in buying land, but real safety comes from the checks before it and the mutation after it. The whole process is below, in order. Fees, forms and online services vary by state, so confirm each step on your state's official site or with the sub-registrar.

1. Checks before the deal (most important)

  • Is the seller's name in the latest khatauni/jamabandi/7-12, and what is their share?
  • Any loan, mortgage, case or stay on the land; is it government/forest/ceiling-category land?
  • The chain of earlier deeds (who bought from whom) and mutation status.
  • Possession and boundaries on the ground; get it measured if needed.

In detail: document checks before buying and the printable checklist.

2. Fix the price and costs

  • Agree the price and area in writing. If the price was quoted per bigha or gaj, check it with the rate calculator.
  • Look up the district circle/DLC rate; stamp duty is charged on it or on the deal price, as state rules say.
  • Estimate stamp duty and registration fee: stamp duty calculator.

3. Draft the sale deed

The deed should clearly state both parties' details, village, khasra/survey number, area, boundaries (neighbours on all four sides), price, mode of payment and handing over of possession. Match every number with the record. Many states let you fill the draft online.

4. Pay stamp duty and fees

Depending on the state, payment is by e-stamp, online challan or stamp paper. Keep the receipt/e-stamp certificate. Always pay through the government portal or an authorised centre.

5. Registration at the sub-registrar office

  • Book an appointment (where available online).
  • Seller, buyer and witnesses attend with ID; photos and thumb/biometric impressions are taken.
  • Read the document and sign. The registered document or its digital copy comes later; keep the acknowledgement.

According to PIB, by April 2023, 28 States/UTs had adopted the National Generic Document Registration System (NGDRS), and over 92% of sub-registrar offices were computerised at the time.

6. After the registry: mutation

The registry creates your title document, but your name enters the khatauni/jamabandi through mutation. Keep checking its status; if it stalls, read mutation pending. Once your name is in, keep a certified copy of the new record.

Common mistakes

  • Paying an advance on verbal assurance without checking the record.
  • Buying joint land without the consent of all co-owners/heirs.
  • Showing a lower price to save duty; this can lead to penalties or disputes later.
  • Forgetting mutation after the registry.

Example

A buyer agrees to purchase 5 biswa in a village. On the portal the khatauni shows the seller's two brothers as co-owners too, so the buyer has all three sign the deed, estimates duty from the circle rate, buys an e-stamp, registers before the sub-registrar and then tracks the mutation.
