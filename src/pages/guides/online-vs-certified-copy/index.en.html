A record seen on a portal is not valid for every purpose. A bank, court or government office often needs a certified copy or a digitally signed copy. The institution you submit to decides which copy it accepts.

Three kinds of copy

  • Information copy: viewed or downloaded online; the portal may mark it "for information only".
  • Certified copy: issued by the revenue office (tehsil/patwari) with signature/seal.
  • Digitally signed copy: issued from the portal with a digital signature where the state offers it.

State examples (as per sources)

  • Maharashtra: an unsigned copy is free; for official work there is a separate paid service for digitally signed 7/12, 8-A etc., which can be verified on the portal.
  • Haryana: news sources say the digitally signed jamabandi (e-Fard) has been available from the portal since May 2023.
  • Rajasthan: one source says the Apna Khata copy is an information copy and banks/courts may need a certified copy from the patwari/tehsil.
  • Punjab: guides disagree on whether a downloaded fard is accepted directly or needs tehsil attestation.

Where sources disagree we do not pick one. The safe method is below.

The safest method

  1. Ask the institution (bank branch, court, office) which copy and in what form it needs.
  2. Get it from the state portal or tehsil as they say.
  3. Pay only on the official portal and keep the receipt.

Avoid fake sites

Be careful of private sites or agents offering "instant certified khatauni". Confirm the real portal address from the state revenue department; each address on our state pages shows its confirmation status.

Example

You want a crop loan. The bank officer says they need a recent khatauni/jamabandi with the tehsil's seal, not a portal printout. Had you gone with a printout you would have had to go back. Asking the institution first is the cheapest step.

Watch the date

Many institutions ask for a recent copy. After a sale or mutation an old copy won't show the new name, so you may need a fresh one.
