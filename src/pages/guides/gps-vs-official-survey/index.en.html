Measuring a field with a phone is easy, but the figure often doesn't match the khatauni area or a neighbour's claim. This page explains why the difference arises, which measurement is good enough for what, and how to get an official measurement (demarcation) when a boundary must be settled.

Three kinds of measurement

  • GPS/map measurement: like our GPS/map tool. Area comes from corner coordinates. Good for a quick estimate.
  • Tape/chain measurement: measure the sides and use the area calculator. On small plots more reliable than GPS, if slope and bunds are handled.
  • Official survey: revenue staff fix the boundary on the ground from the village map and record. This report is what counts in disputes, partitions and courts.

Why GPS figures differ

  • GPS accuracy: even in the open a phone can drift a few metres; more under trees, near walls or in cloud. Our tool shows ± metres per corner.
  • Where corners are placed: middle, inside or outside of the bund adds a few feet on every side.
  • Small plots: a 5 m error matters little on an acre, a lot on a 200-gaj plot.
  • Old record: the khatauni area comes from an older survey or partition; bunds may have shifted since.
  • Units: the record may be in hectares or a local unit; convert before comparing.

When each is enough

  • A rough figure before buying, farm planning, price arithmetic: GPS or tape.
  • A boundary quarrel, an encroachment claim, partition, doubt about the area in a registry: official survey.

General path to an official demarcation

  1. Apply at the tehsil or on the state portal (where online). States call it paimaish, seemankan, hadbandi or demarcation. For a structure use the application drafts tool.
  2. Attach the current khatauni/jamabandi, khasra number, village name and ID; pay the fee as per state rules and keep the receipt.
  3. Neighbouring holders are notified so they can be present.
  4. Revenue staff measure on the ground from the official map and mark the boundary.
  5. Get a copy of the demarcation report/map; if you disagree, objection or appeal routes are in state rules.

Looking at your state's official village map first helps: where to see land maps.

How our calculation works

The GPS tool converts latitude/longitude to metres with the mean Earth radius (6,371 km) and applies the shoelace formula. Units follow fixed definitions: 1 ft = 0.3048 m. Even with correct maths, the result is only as good as the corners placed.

Example

A farmer measured a field by GPS at 1.8 bigha while the khatauni said 2 bigha. Suspecting a neighbour had moved the bund, they applied for demarcation at the tehsil instead of quarrelling. The official survey showed one bund had indeed moved inwards, and boundary marks were reset from the report.
