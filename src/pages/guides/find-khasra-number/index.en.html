The most useful key for finding a record is the khasra (gata/survey) number. If you don't have it, try the methods below in order. This is general guidance; portals and processes differ by state.

1. Check the papers you have

  • Sale deed/registry: usually lists the khasra/survey number, village and area.
  • An old khatauni/jamabandi/7-12 copy or inheritance papers.
  • Land revenue or tax receipts, bank or crop-loan papers, electricity connection papers with the field's address.

2. Family and old records

Partition or inheritance papers, copies of old cases and documents kept by elders often give the khasra number.

3. Local revenue staff

Ask the village revenue official (patwari/lekhpal/talathi etc., the title varies by state) for the khasra number, describing the field's location. Bring the holder's name, village and old deed details.

4. Name search on the portal

Many state portals allow name search, but not everywhere or for every record. Spellings vary, so try two or three forms and pick the village correctly. Get your portal address from the state pages.

5. Identify on the ground if needed

If papers and land don't match, ask the tehsil about seeing the map or demarcation. Demarcation process, fees and time differ by state and we have not verified them.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the everyday village name when the revenue record uses another name.
  • Taking a neighbour's khasra as yours.
  • Confusing the khata number with the khasra number; see the difference here.

Example

You have your grandfather's 1985 deed listing the village, khasra number and area. On the portal choose district, tehsil and that village and enter the same khasra number. If today's record shows a different name, look at the mutations in between; the same khasra number shows the chain.
