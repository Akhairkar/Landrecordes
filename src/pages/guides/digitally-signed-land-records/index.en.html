Many states issue PDFs of records such as the khatauni, jamabandi or 7/12 that carry the digital signature of an authorised officer or system. Such a file differs from a plain screen copy, but it helps only if the file stays in its original form and the signature can be checked. This page explains what a digital signature is, how to check it, and which mistakes make it useless.

What a digital signature does

  • Who issued it: the signature carries a digital certificate naming the officer or body that signed the file.
  • Detects changes: if even one character changes after signing, the check shows the signature as invalid.
  • When it was signed: the signature details include date and time.

Three kinds of copies

  • Screen view / screenshot: for information; no signature.
  • Digitally signed PDF: can be verified as the original file; where it is accepted depends on the state and the receiving institution.
  • Office-certified copy: paper copy with seal/signature.

In detail: online vs certified copy.

How to check a signature in a PDF (general method)

  1. Open the file in a PDF reader that shows digital signatures (many desktop and mobile readers have a "Signature panel" or signature details option).
  2. Tap/click the signature to see details: signer's name/designation, certifying authority, date.
  3. Look for a message like "Document has not been modified since signing", or a "modified" warning.
  4. "Validity unknown" usually reflects trust settings, not necessarily a fake file; still match it against any verification code/QR on the portal.
  5. Match the khasra/khata, names and date inside the record with the current information on the portal.

Mistakes that break the signature

  • Sending a screenshot of the PDF.
  • Making a new file by "edit", "compress" or "print to PDF" in some app.
  • Handing over only a printout when the receiver needed the digital file.

Safest: email/upload the original file downloaded from the portal, and first ask the institution whether it accepts the digital file, a print with a code, or an office-certified copy.

By state

Whether digitally signed records are offered, their fee and their acceptance vary by state. Open your portal from the state page or the record finder and follow its instructions. We will state a state's fee or rule here only once verified from a government source.

Example

Someone downloaded a digitally signed 7/12 PDF for a bank loan but "compressed" it in a phone app before sending. The bank's check showed the signature invalid. Downloading the original again and sending it unchanged showed a valid signature and the work moved on.
