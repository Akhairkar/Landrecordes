Recording a new name in the revenue record after a purchase, inheritance or partition is called mutation, dakhil-kharij, namantaran, intkal or ferfar depending on the state. This page maps the whole path: which stage you are at, what to look for there, and which guide or tool helps next.

The whole path in five stages

  1. Application: apply on the basis of the sale deed, will or succession and always keep the acknowledgement. In some states registration starts the process automatically; elsewhere you apply separately. For a structure use the application drafts tool.
  2. Inquiry and notice: the village revenue official (patwari/lekhpal/talathi) reports; many states publish a notice so objections can be filed.
  3. Objection (if any): the case goes to a hearing. How to object or answer someone's objection: objections and rejection.
  4. Order: the competent revenue officer approves or rejects.
  5. Entry in the record: after approval the name appears in the new khatauni/jamabandi/7-12. The final check is that the name, father's/husband's name and share are right in the new record.

Common status words and their usual meaning

Portal wording differs by state. These are general meanings; confirm the exact sense on your state portal or at the tehsil.

  • Received / Registered: the application is in the system; inquiry may not have started.
  • Under inquiry / For report: the revenue official's report is pending.
  • Objection / Disputed: someone objected; a hearing follows.
  • Hearing date: appear or reply on that date.
  • Disposed: the case is decided; the order shows approval or rejection.
  • Approved: order to enter the name; still check the new record.
  • Rejected: get the reasons in writing and see the next route.

Which situation are you in?

  • The application has sat at one stage for long: mutation pending.
  • Heirs' names after a death: inheritance guide.
  • Name entered but spelling, share or area wrong: correcting a record.
  • Someone else's name is about to go on your land, or your application was rejected: objections and rejection.
  • What mutation is called in your state: names for mutation.

Papers to keep at every stage

  • Acknowledgement and case/application number.
  • A copy of the basis document: sale deed, will or death certificate.
  • Every notice, hearing date and a certified copy of the order.
  • Copies of the old and new record, as proof of the change.

Example

After a registry someone saw "Disposed" on the portal and assumed the name was entered. Two months later the new khatauni still showed the old name. The order copy revealed the application had been rejected for a missing document. The right move was to read the order as soon as it said "Disposed" and take the next step from the objections and rejection guide.
