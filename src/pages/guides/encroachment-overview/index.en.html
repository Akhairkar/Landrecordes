This is general educational information, not legal advice. In encroachment cases timing, documents and state law matter a lot, so consult a qualified lawyer and the local revenue office early. We are not stating any legal section, time limit or outcome here.

First: don't remove anyone by force

Breaking locks, violence or bulldozing can create a case against you and worsen the situation. Moving through legal and administrative channels is considered the safe route.

1. Prepare proof of your rights

  • Sale deed or proof of succession.
  • Recent khatauni/jamabandi/7-12 and mutation papers (what is mutation).
  • Tax/rent receipts and the map.

2. Collect evidence of the encroachment

  • Dated photos/videos, witnesses' names and contacts.
  • A timeline of when and how the occupation began.
  • Copies of any earlier complaints.

3. Written complaint and administration

People usually give a written application to the tehsil/revenue officer, the local police station and (if there is construction) the municipal body/panchayat, and keep the acknowledgement. Which officer handles what depends on the state and the facts.

4. Legal options

A lawyer can say whether you need relief in court such as recovery of possession or an injunction. Delay can hurt in possession cases, so seek advice early.

What the application should include

  • Your name, address, contact and land details (village, khasra/survey, area).
  • The encroacher's name (if known) and how the occupation happened.
  • A list of your documents and enclosures.
  • The action you seek, in plain words.

Keep in mind

  • Government, shared or ancestral land cases can be different.
  • The occupier may claim rights too; evidence becomes even more important then.
