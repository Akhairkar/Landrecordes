Buying land is a big decision. Don't rely on a single record or the seller's word. Below is a general checklist; documents and rules vary by state and this is not legal advice.

1. Is the seller really the owner?

  • Check the seller's name and share in the revenue record (khatauni/jamabandi/7-12).
  • The chain of previous deeds: how did the seller get the land (purchase, inheritance, gift)?
  • If there are several holders, will all of them consent and sign?

2. Do the record and deed match?

Match khasra, khata, area, village and boundaries in both. Check whether mutations are complete.

3. Encumbrances and disputes

  • Ask about loans, mortgages or court cases and check the relevant records (where they are kept differs by state).
  • Read other rights or entries recorded in records such as the 7/12.

4. Land type and use

Agricultural, residential or other; building on or changing the use of agricultural land may need separate permission, and many states restrict purchase of agricultural land. These rules are state-specific, so ask a local officer or lawyer.

5. Possession and boundaries

  • Visit and see who is in possession.
  • Match the map and measurements with the ground; ask the tehsil about demarcation if needed.
  • Confirm access road and drainage rights.

6. Tax and other receipts

Ask for recent land revenue/municipal tax receipts and utility bills.

7. Finally

Talk about payment and registration only after all checks. Avoid cash and keep a receipt for every payment. A printable checklist is here.

Who can help

  • Lawyer: to check the chain of documents and legal risk.
  • Tehsil/revenue staff: to understand the record and map.
  • Surveyor: to match boundaries and measurements on the ground.

Warning signs

  • The seller is in a hurry or avoids showing original documents.
  • The price is far below the local average.
  • The record shows someone else's name and the seller talks of a "power of attorney" or "the name will be entered later".
  • Someone else is farming or building on the land.

Check the area

Convert the area in the deed with the calculator and match it with the record, and check a measured field with the plot area tool. Small differences can come from measuring; question big ones.
