Which record should you choose in Pune?

  • Property card / malmatta patrak: This may be relevant for city-surveyed property. Look for the CTS or city-survey number in your documents. A postal address alone does not guarantee that a record can be found.
  • 7/12 extract and 8-A: In Maharashtra, these are common starting points for rural agricultural land records. Select the district, taluka, village and survey/gat number carefully.
  • Measurement or boundary checks: For measurement-related needs, use the state's eMojni portal and the relevant land-records office. Online maps and calculators cannot certify a legal boundary.
  • Older documents: If the current record is not enough, check whether the department's e-Records/archived-document service is available. Not every older document is necessarily online.

Pune district land-records office

Pune district administration's official View Property Card page lists the Superintendent of Land Records office at Government Bungalow No. 1, Samata Nagar, Airport Road, Badami Chowk, near Ishanya Mall, Yerawada, Pune 411006, phone 020-26684658. Before visiting, recheck the official contact page, as office details can change.

Online viewing versus a certified copy

Viewing a record on screen is not the same as obtaining a copy accepted by a bank, court, registry or government application. Maharashtra's portal may separate view-only and digitally signed services. Ask the institution receiving the document which copy it accepts. Do not infer fees or availability from this guide; check the current official portal.

If the record does not appear

  1. Check the official district, taluka and village/locality name.
  2. Where supported, search with the survey/gat or CTS number instead of only a name.
  3. Choose the right service for rural 7/12 versus an urban property card.
  4. For older documents or measurement requests, use the district land-records office or the state's official service page.
