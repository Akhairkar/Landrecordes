The khatauni is a holding-wise land record: the holders of one khata (holding), their shares and all the khasra/gata plots in that holding appear together. The word is commonly used in Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand and Madhya Pradesh.

Main parts of a khatauni

  1. Khata number: identifies the holding.
  2. Holders and shares: each holder's share where there are several.
  3. Khasra/gata list: every plot in the holding with its area.
  4. Land type or remarks: e.g. irrigated/unirrigated or a special entry.

Most common reading mistakes

  • Assuming the holding's total area is all yours when your share may be smaller.
  • Misreading one digit of the khasra number and opening someone else's land.
  • Choosing the wrong village; one tehsil can have several similarly named villages.
  • Ignoring the unit of area (bigha, hectare, square metre).

Khatauni vs jamabandi vs 7/12

The names differ but the purpose is similar: whose name the land is recorded in. Khatauni and jamabandi show the whole holding by khata, while Maharashtra's 7/12 shows one survey number and the holding summary is in 8-A. Compare in the glossary tool.

Copies of the khatauni

The khatauni you see online and the certified copy a bank or court asks for may be different. See online vs certified copy.

An example

Suppose khata 45 has two brothers as holders and three khasras. The khatauni shows khata 45, both names, each share (say half each) and the area of all three khasras. If you are buying only one khasra, it must be clear whose share it falls in; otherwise the sale may reach the other holders' share. The khatauni only shows what is recorded; who actually possesses which part after a partition must be checked on the ground.

Unit of area

Area may be written in bigha-biswa, acres or hectares. Read the total only after checking the unit and convert with the calculator. Bigha-biswa varies by place, so enter your local value.
