In Maharashtra's city-surveyed (urban) areas, land is recorded on the property card (malmatta patrak). A card is made for each city survey number (CTS). According to the district administration's site, the property card is also available on Mahabhulekh.

One third-party guide says 8-A is rural and the property card urban; this is a general observation, not an official rule.

Where to find the CTS number

  • On the property card or 7/12.
  • For apartments, often in the agreement/registered deed.
  • If unsure, ask the local CTS/city survey office.

How to view it online

In Mahabhulekh's free section choose your division, pick property card, then district, taluka/office, village/city and search by CTS number or name. Menus change, so follow what the portal shows. The portal also mentions a separate digitally signed copy for legal use.

Before buying

Match the name, area and any encumbrance on the card with the registered deed and society/builder documents. The checklist can help. We have not verified Maharashtra's fees or timelines.

Common words in urban records

  • City survey number (CTS): the number given to each plot in the city survey.
  • Malmatta patrak: the Marathi name for the property card.
  • Holder and area: recorded holder and area in square metres, which you can convert with the calculator.

For flat buyers

A separate property card is not usually made for each flat; typically the card is for the whole land or society, and your share rests on the registered agreement and society/builder documents. Ask the CTS office or a lawyer for the exact position.

If the card and records differ

If names, area or numbers differ, see the correction guide and ask the office about the correction process.
