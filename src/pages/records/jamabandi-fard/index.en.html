The jamabandi is the Record of Rights in states such as Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan, Himachal, Jammu & Kashmir and Bihar. A copy taken from it is called a fard or nakal in many states.

Words you will meet in a jamabandi

  • Khewat: the ownership/holding unit listing co-sharers.
  • Khatauni: cultivator details (shown with the khewat in Haryana-Punjab).
  • Khasra: plot number.
  • Intkal: mutation; after it is entered the new name appears in the jamabandi.
  • Girdawari: periodic crop and possession inspection record, reported to be available with the jamabandi on the J&K portal.

Fard and nakal: how valid is a copy?

This varies by state and sources do not always agree:

  • Haryana: news sources say the digitally signed jamabandi (e-Fard) has been available from the jamabandi portal since 6 May 2023.
  • Rajasthan: one source says the Apna Khata copy is an information copy and a bank or court may need a certified copy from the patwari/tehsil.
  • Punjab: some guides say a downloaded fard with QR code and digital signature is accepted directly; others say tehsil attestation is needed. Ask the receiving institution first.
  • Himachal: the district administration's site says jamabandi and shajra nasab copies are issued through HimBhoomi for a set fee.

We have not verified any state's fee, so check the current fee on the state portal before paying.

Reading tips

  • Check the jamabandi year; an old jamabandi will not show recent intkals.
  • Read the remarks column; some sources say changes are first noted there.
  • Convert units such as kanal/marla with the calculator.

An example

In one village, khewat 12 lists four co-sharers and several khasras. If you are buying a share in only one khasra, check in the jamabandi which khewat/khatauni that khasra falls in and who the co-sharers are with what shares. The fard gives the same information as a copy. Only then do the sale deed and intkal move forward.
