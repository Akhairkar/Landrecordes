Bhu-Aadhaar, or ULPIN (Unique Land Parcel Identification Number), is a unique ID for a land parcel. News sources describe it as a 14-digit number being assigned to parcels under the Department of Land Resources' Digital India Land Records Modernisation Programme (DILRMP).

What it is, and what it is not

  • Is: an identity for one piece of land, so the same parcel can be linked across states' and departments' systems.
  • Is not: an owner's ID card, proof of ownership or a substitute for Aadhaar. It numbers land, not people.

What is reported about DILRMP 3.0

News articles say DILRMP 3.0 (2026–2031) guidelines were issued in September 2026, describing a central scheme of about ₹565.5 crore and state land stacks. Sources do not fully agree on details such as the launch date, so check these figures on the Department of Land Resources' official site.

Where to find your Bhu-Aadhaar

State-wise availability and display may differ and we found no verified state-wise status. Some state records or portals may show the number. The safest way is to ask your state portal/tehsil; open your portal with the record finder.

Avoiding fraud

  • Do not pay private sites or agents that offer to "make a Bhu-Aadhaar card"; numbering is a government process.
  • Stop if any site asks for your OTP, Aadhaar or bank details.

What it could help with

In principle one permanent ID per parcel makes it easier to link revenue, registration and map records and may reduce duplicate or fake records. In practice states are adopting it at different speeds, so check how far it is used in your state on the state portal.

Relation to the khasra number

Bhu-Aadhaar does not replace the khasra/survey number. The khasra identifies land within a village, while Bhu-Aadhaar aims to identify a parcel nationally. Record searches still generally use the state's khasra/khata; see what is a khasra number.
