A Record of Rights (RoR) is the revenue department's record of a village's land: who is recorded on it, the area and the type of land. In Uttar Pradesh it appears as the khatauni, in Rajasthan and Punjab as the jamabandi, and in Maharashtra and Gujarat as the 7/12.

What it usually contains

  • Village, tehsil and district
  • Plot/survey number (identifies the piece of land) and khata/khewat number (identifies the holding)
  • Area as recorded (rakba) and its unit
  • Name and share of each recorded holder or cultivator
  • Land type (irrigated, unirrigated, abadi etc.) and, in many states, crop or possession details

The exact form, columns and words differ by state, so do not read one state's khatauni through another state's jamabandi template. See your state page for the local form.

Why the same thing has many names

Land revenue is a state subject and each state has its own history, so names, forms and portals differ. The national DILRMP programme digitises state records and keeps a directory of portals, but it does not make every state use the same names or forms. RoR vs sale deed vs mutation

DocumentWhat it is
Sale deed / registryThe registered transfer document, recorded at the sub-registrar's office.
MutationA separate process that changes the name in the revenue record. Registration alone does not update the revenue record.
Record of RightsThe revenue record that shows the new name after mutation.

Caution

Revenue records are mainly records of land revenue, possession and cultivation. Ownership disputes are generally decided by courts and competent authorities, so do not treat an online record alone as settling ownership. Before big decisions such as a purchase, match several documents; see what to check before buying land.
