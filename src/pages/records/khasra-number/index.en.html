A khasra number identifies one separate piece of land (plot/field) on the village revenue map. In Uttar Pradesh it is also called the gata number; in Maharashtra and Gujarat it is the survey/gat/block number, and in Bihar the khesra.

Khasra vs khata

The khasra identifies a piece of land, while the khata/khewat is the number of a holder's holding. One khata can contain several khasras and one khasra can have several holders. To identify your land in the record, match both the right khasra and the right khata.

How sub-divisions are written

After a partition or sale a khasra may be split, and the record then shows sub-numbers such as 123/1 and 123/2 or separate areas. The exact style varies by state; always type the number into the portal exactly as it appears in the record.

Why it is the best search key

  • Name spellings change between Hindi and English and across records; the khasra number does not.
  • Name search is not available everywhere, while plot-number search usually is.
  • A village's revenue name can differ from its everyday name, so pick the village carefully.

If you don't know your khasra number

Check an old sale deed, tax/rent receipts, bank papers or family records, or ask the local patwari/lekhpal/talathi. Detailed ways are in this guide.

Use it with the calculator

See which unit the area against the khasra is written in, then convert it to acres, guntha or square feet with the calculator. For bigha or biswa you must enter your local value.

Example

A deed says "village Rampur, khasra 123/2, area 0.405 hectare". That is the second part of khasra 123, about 1 acre (1 hectare ≈ 2.4711 acres). On the portal, choose village Rampur and enter 123/2 or the form your state uses.
