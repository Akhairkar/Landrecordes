In Maharashtra and Gujarat the main record of rural land is the 7/12 extract (satbara). The 8-A is a summary of a holder's holding. According to the district administration's site, the 7/12, 8-A and property card are available online on Mahabhulekh.

The two halves of a 7/12

The name comes from village registers Form 7 and Form 12. Form 7 records land and rights (holders, area, other rights); Form 12 records crops and cultivation. Area is usually written in hectare-are; convert it to acres or guntha with the calculator.

What 8-A shows

8-A is a khata-wise summary of all survey/gat numbers in a holder's name, useful for seeing a person's total holding.

Ferfar and rights entries

Changes after a sale, inheritance or encumbrance are recorded as ferfar (mutation entries) and linked to the 7/12. Before buying, read the "other rights" and ferfar parts; the talathi or tehsil office can explain entries you don't understand.

Free copy vs digitally signed copy

  • An unsigned copy is in the free services and is for information only.
  • For official or legal use there is a separate paid service for digitally signed copies; the district site lists a separate address (digitalsatbara) for it.
  • A signed copy can be verified on the portal with its verification number.

Urban land: the property card

In city-surveyed areas the property card is used instead of the 7/12. See property card and CTS number.

In Gujarat

Gujarat also uses 7/12 and 8-A through the AnyRoR portal, and hakk-patrak entries show changes in rights. Details are on the Gujarat page.

A simple reading order

  1. Match village, taluka and district at the top.
  2. Check the survey/gat number and sub-division.
  3. Read the area and its unit (hectare-are).
  4. Check the holder's name and khata number.
  5. Read other rights and ferfar entries.
  6. Check the crop part to see what is actually grown.
