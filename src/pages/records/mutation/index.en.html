Mutation is the process that records a new name in the revenue record after a sale, inheritance or partition. Its name changes by state: dakhil-kharij in Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Uttarakhand and Jharkhand; namantaran in Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh; intkal in Haryana, Punjab, Himachal and Jammu & Kashmir; ferfar in Maharashtra.

Why mutation after registration

Registration records the transfer, but the revenue record (khatauni/jamabandi/7-12) changes only when mutation is entered. In Bihar, for example, a new name appears in the jamabandi only after dakhil-kharij.

When mutation is needed

  • After a sale or gift
  • Inheritance (heirs' names after a death)
  • Partition
  • Name changes after other court/government orders

What to keep ready, generally

Usually the transfer document or proof of succession, identity and address proof, a copy of the current record and an application. The exact list, fee and timeline depend on state rules and we have not verified them; get the current list from the state portal or tehsil.

If it is stuck

Keep the receipt and check the status on the portal or at the tehsil. Steps are in what to do if mutation is pending.

Inheritance vs purchase

For a purchase the new name is sought on the basis of the deed; for inheritance the heirs' names are entered after a death. Inheritance usually brings all heirs and their shares into view, so settling family agreement and documents first makes it easier. Who is entitled to how much depends on law and facts; ask a lawyer or revenue officer.

After applying

  • Keep the acknowledgement or application number.
  • Many states have a notice or public notification for objections; its period and form depend on state rules.
  • After the order, the name may take time to appear; check the record again and compare with the old name.
